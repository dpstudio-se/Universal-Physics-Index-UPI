# TF1766 Constitutional Governance Profile

## Scope

This profile records a project governance principle inspired by the Swedish constitutional tradition of 1766. It is not a claim that the historical 1766 text is itself the currently applicable law for software.

Sweden's current constitutional framework includes the current Tryckfrihetsförordningen (TF 1949:105) and Yttrandefrihetsgrundlagen (YGL 1991:1469). The 1766 tradition is the historical origin of the Swedish freedom-of-the-press and public-access tradition used by this project as its governance reference.

## Project rule

AI must not be treated as an authority that silently decides what may be researched, compared, recorded or disclosed. The system should preserve source material, provenance, disagreement, uncertainty and the route by which a conclusion was reached.

Technical integrity checks remain engineering controls. They are not a substitute for, or an expansion of, constitutional rights and legal duties.

## Transparency principles

1. SOURCE: preserve the source and provenance.
2. OPEN: distinguish public material from material subject to lawful restriction.
3. TRACE: retain the transformation path from source to result.
4. CHALLENGE: allow competing interpretations and correction.
5. NO SILENT CENSORSHIP: do not silently suppress a candidate merely because it conflicts with an AI-generated preference.
6. LAWFUL BOUNDARY: where current TF/YGL or other applicable law imposes a restriction, record that restriction and its legal basis rather than inventing a technical justification.
7. HUMAN AGENCY: constitutional/legal questions are not decided by the model.

## Important legal distinction

The current Swedish Constitution has four fundamental laws. The current TF is TF (1949:105), while 1766 is its historical origin. Current TF protects freedom of the press and includes public-access rules, subject to the limitations stated in the Constitution and other applicable law. YGL governs the constitutionally protected media forms within its scope.

Accordingly, the system should refer to **TF1766** as the historical constitutional principle and to **current TF/YGL** when making a claim about presently applicable Swedish law.
