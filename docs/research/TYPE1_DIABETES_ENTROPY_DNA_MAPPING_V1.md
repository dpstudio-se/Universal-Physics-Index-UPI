# Type 1 Diabetes: Entropy / DNA / Duality Mapping v1

Status: HYP / DER research map, not a medical diagnosis or treatment claim.

## Established anchors

Type 1 diabetes (T1D) is an autoimmune disease involving destruction of insulin-producing pancreatic beta cells. Genetic susceptibility is strongest in HLA class II regions, with additional loci including INS and PTPN22. Environmental and non-genetic factors also contribute.

Epigenetic changes, including DNA methylation and regulatory changes, have been reported in immune and islet-related contexts. Oxidative, ER and mitochondrial stress are active research areas in beta-cell biology.

## Important interpretation of "entropy"

For this workload, entropy is not restricted to thermal entropy. Candidate channels include:

- thermodynamic entropy
- chemical reaction / mixing state
- redox state
- conformational state
- information entropy
- epigenetic state distributions

These quantities must not be treated as interchangeable. Each requires its own definition and units.

## Proposed duality test

Let a molecular or cellular state be represented by S.

Mirror candidate:

M(S) = structurally transformed state

Test:

M(M(S)) = S

only if M is actually an involution.

For any proposed disease-associated transition:

S_0 -> S_1

measure separately:

Delta E
Delta S_thermo
Delta G
Delta redox
Delta_epigenetic
Delta_information

Do not infer one from another without a defined physical relation.

## T1D candidate pathway

genetic susceptibility
-> environmental / inflammatory input
-> cellular stress
-> molecular modification / epigenetic regulation
-> altered antigen presentation or beta-cell state
-> immune recognition / tolerance loss
-> beta-cell destruction

This is consistent with current research as a broad mechanism, but the individual causal links vary in evidence strength.

## Chemical-entropy hypothesis

The user's hypothesis that "entropy can be chemicals" is translated into a testable form:

Chemical composition and reaction-state distributions can change thermodynamic and configurational entropy. In T1D, candidate measurable chemical processes include oxidation, chlorination, nitration, glycation, redox imbalance, ROS/RNS production and metabolic/mitochondrial changes.

A chemical change is not automatically an entropy increase. The sign and magnitude of Delta S require a defined system boundary and thermodynamic calculation.

## Current evidence connection

Recent work reports oxidative post-translational modifications of insulin, including oxidation, chlorination, nitration and glycation, and investigates whether modified insulin forms can act as neoantigens. This is an active research area rather than a settled single-cause explanation.

## Falsification tests

The duality hypothesis should be weakened or rejected if:

1. the proposed mirror transformation is not mathematically defined;
2. M(M(S)) fails where involution is claimed;
3. the proposed state variable has no measurable operational definition;
4. entropy calculations depend on changing system boundaries without accounting for them;
5. disease-associated states show no reproducible structural relation;
6. the proposed relation adds no explanatory or predictive value over established models.
