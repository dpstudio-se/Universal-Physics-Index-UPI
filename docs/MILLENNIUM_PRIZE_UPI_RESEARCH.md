# Millennium Prize Problems × UPI Research Program

Status: HYP / STOP where unresolved. This document is a research plan, not a claim that any Millennium Prize problem has been solved.

## Goal

Use the Universal Physics Index (UPI) as a governed reference, provenance, transformation, and verification layer while attempting independent mathematical progress on the Millennium Prize Problems.

UPI must not manufacture evidence. Established results remain EST, derived results DER, testable proposals HYP, unresolved boundaries STOP, invalid/contradicted claims ERR, and symbolic mappings SYM.

## Problem tracks

### 1. Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness
Target: determine whether the 3D incompressible Navier–Stokes equations with smooth divergence-free initial data have global smooth finite-energy solutions, or exhibit a valid breakdown.

UPI support:
- canonical equations, dimensions, assumptions and known regularity results
- transformation/invariant checks
- energy estimates and dimensional analysis
- candidate singularity mechanisms
- symbolic/numerical experiments kept separate from proof
- independent proof audit and Lean formalization where possible

Current external status: OpenAI announced a claimed finite-time singularity proof on 2026-09-08. UPI must record this as an external claim requiring independent mathematical review, not as EST. Clay's prize rules require publication in a qualifying outlet, two years since publication, and general mathematical acceptance before prize consideration.

### 2. Riemann Hypothesis
Target: prove every nontrivial zero of zeta has real part 1/2, or produce a valid counterexample.

UPI support:
- exact definitions and functional equation
- zero-data provenance
- equivalent criteria
- spectral/operator formulations
- explicit-formula transformations
- numerical evidence versus proof separation
- adversarial search for counterexamples and proof gaps

### 3. P versus NP
Target: determine whether P = NP or P != NP.

UPI support:
- complexity definitions and reductions
- formal encoding of decision/search/verification problems
- proof-obligation graph
- barrier tracking
- reduction-chain verification
- complexity-preserving transformation checks
- formal proof artifacts

### 4. Yang–Mills existence and mass gap
Target: construct a mathematically rigorous quantum Yang–Mills theory on R^4 for a compact simple gauge group and prove a positive mass gap.

UPI support:
- gauge symmetry and dimensional consistency
- classical-to-quantum mappings
- constructive/QFT axioms
- renormalization and scaling records
- spectral-gap candidates
- lattice evidence versus continuum proof
- theorem dependency graph

### 5. Hodge Conjecture
Target: establish that Hodge classes of the required type are rational linear combinations of algebraic cycle classes for smooth projective complex varieties.

UPI support:
- algebraic/topological definitions
- cohomology and Hodge decomposition
- known special cases
- cycle-class maps
- dimensional/special-case decomposition
- counterexample search in bounded families
- exact proof dependency tracking

### 6. Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer Conjecture
Target: relate the order of vanishing of the L-function at s=1 to the rank of the elliptic curve's rational points.

UPI support:
- elliptic-curve invariants
- Mordell–Weil rank
- L-function definitions
- local/global factorization
- computational experiments with explicit curves
- theorem dependency graph
- strict separation of numerical evidence and proof

### 7. Poincare Conjecture
Status: EST / solved by Perelman. Do not treat it as an open prize problem.

Use it as a calibration track:
- Ricci flow
- geometric decomposition
- proof architecture
- formalization/audit techniques

## UPI research protocol

For every proposed step:
1. State the exact mathematical problem.
2. Retrieve canonical UPI definitions and known results.
3. Generate candidate transformations/derivations.
4. Preserve every assumption explicitly.
5. Mark every claim EST, DER, HYP, STOP, ERR, or SYM.
6. Run algebraic, numerical, dimensional and logical checks where applicable.
7. Attempt adversarial counterexamples.
8. Identify the first unresolved proof obligation.
9. Store provenance and external references.
10. Never promote a hypothesis to EST because it resembles an established result.

## Cross-problem search

After each track is analyzed independently, compare only mathematically justified structures:
- invariants
- conservation laws
- symmetry
- duality
- scaling
- spectral structure
- fixed points
- singularity formation
- compactness/coercivity
- transformation/composition laws

A cross-problem analogy is SYM until a DER chain establishes an actual mathematical relation.

## Success criterion

The project succeeds even without solving all seven if UPI produces reproducible derivations, machine-checkable proof obligations, explicit stopping points, independently testable conjectures, or new rigorous partial results.

A claimed complete solution must survive independent verification and the applicable Clay rules.
